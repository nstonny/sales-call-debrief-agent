SALES CALL TRANSCRIPT 2
========================
Industry: Commercial Insurance
Call Type: Closing Call
Outcome: Deal closed
Duration: 34 minutes
Rep: Damon Okafor
Prospect: Helena Strauss, COO, Strauss Architecture & Design (62 employees)
========================

Damon: Helena, good morning. So we're at the point where I think we have everything we need on our end — I just want to walk you through the final proposal and make sure you're comfortable before we get anything signed.

Helena: Yes, let's do it. I've looked at the summary you sent. I have a couple of questions.

Damon: Of course, go ahead.

Helena: The professional liability limit — you've got it at $2 million. Last year we had it at $1 million and our broker said it was fine. Why are you recommending $2 million?

Damon: Good question. A couple of reasons. Your revenue has grown by about 40% since your last renewal, which means your project values are higher. If a client makes a claim tied to a design error on a $4 million build, a $1 million limit could leave you exposed. The second reason is that some of the municipal contracts you mentioned you're targeting — the public sector work — they often require $2 million as a minimum. We'd hate for you to win a bid and then find out you can't actually sign the contract.

Helena: Hmm. That's a fair point actually. I hadn't thought about the municipal requirement.

Damon: It comes up more than people expect. And the difference in premium is about $1,800 annually, which relative to the exposure I think is worth it — but ultimately it's your call.

Helena: No, I think you're right. Let's keep the $2 million. My other question is about the cyber liability rider. We've never had that before. Is that something you genuinely think we need, or is it just an upsell?

Damon: Honest answer? For a firm your size, with client files, CAD drawings, financial data — yes, I do think it's worth it. You're not a huge target, but small firms get hit too, often because attackers know you're less likely to have enterprise-level security. The rider covers things like ransomware recovery costs, client notification if their data is compromised, and legal fees if you get sued. It's $640 a year. If you get hit once, you'll wish you had it. If you never get hit, you'll think it was a waste — that's how insurance works.

Helena: [laughs] Fair enough. Alright. I'm comfortable with both. What does the next step look like?

Damon: I'll send you the formal binder today. You'll sign that electronically, and the coverage goes into effect 48 hours after we receive it. Your old policy lapses on the 18th, so we have a comfortable window. I'll also send you a summary certificate you can share with clients or anyone who needs proof of coverage.

Helena: Perfect. And if I have questions once I'm reading through the actual policy document?

Damon: You call me directly. My number's on everything. I'm also going to put a 90-day check-in in my calendar to make sure everything's landing well.

Helena: That's great, Damon. Honestly, the last broker I had I never heard from again after the policy was signed.

Damon: That's unfortunately pretty common in this industry. Okay, I'll get that binder to you within the hour. Congratulations on the renewal, and I hope year two is even better than the first.

Helena: Thanks very much.
