SALES CALL TRANSCRIPT 1
========================
Industry: SaaS / Project Management Software
Call Type: Discovery Call
Outcome: Follow-up scheduled
Duration: 22 minutes
Rep: Sarah Chen
Prospect: Marcus Webb, VP of Operations, Meridian Logistics (280 employees)
========================

Sarah: Hi Marcus, thanks for making time today. I know you mentioned you only had about 20 minutes, so I'll keep us on track. Before I jump in, is that still the case?

Marcus: Yeah, I've got a hard stop at 2:30, so let's keep it tight.

Sarah: Perfect. So, you filled out the form on our site last week — it said you were looking at project management tools. Can you tell me a bit about what's driving that right now?

Marcus: Sure. We're growing fast — we went from about 180 people to 280 in the last 18 months — and right now we're basically running everything out of spreadsheets and email. It's... not pretty.

Sarah: I can imagine. When you say "everything," what does that look like day to day? What kinds of projects are we talking about?

Marcus: Mainly cross-functional stuff. Our ops team coordinates with finance, procurement, and the warehouse teams on basically every major initiative. Right now, nobody knows what anyone else is working on. We had a situation last month where two teams were independently solving the same problem and didn't find out until they both presented solutions to the CFO in the same meeting.

Sarah: Oh wow.

Marcus: Yeah, it was not a great look.

Sarah: I can understand why that would accelerate the search. So it sounds like visibility is the core pain — knowing who's doing what across teams. Is there anything else beyond that?

Marcus: Accountability, honestly. Projects just... stall. Nobody's really on the hook because everything lives in someone's inbox. By the time we notice something's slipping, we've already missed a deadline.

Sarah: Got it. So visibility and accountability. Have you looked at anything already, or is this early stage?

Marcus: We looked at Asana about a year ago and it felt like overkill. Too complex for what we needed. And then one of our team leads trialled Monday.com for about six weeks but people just didn't adopt it.

Sarah: That's really useful context. What do you think happened with the Monday adoption?

Marcus: Honestly? Nobody made it mandatory. The team lead liked it, but the rest of the team kept doing things the old way and after a while he just gave up.

Sarah: So change management is going to be as important as the tool itself.

Marcus: Exactly. If I go to my CEO with another platform and it fails, that's on me.

Sarah: That's fair. I don't want to oversell you — what I can say is that rollout support is something we take seriously. We have an onboarding team that works with your ops lead to build the initial structure, and we don't go live until the team has actually used it in a real workflow. But I'd want to understand your setup more before promising anything specific. Can we schedule a proper 45-minute call this week where I bring in one of our solutions engineers?

Marcus: Yeah, that works. Thursday afternoon is open.

Sarah: Perfect, I'll send a calendar invite. One last thing — who else would typically be involved in a decision like this?

Marcus: Probably my CFO. And the ops team lead, Jamie Reyes.

Sarah: Great. I'll include slots for all three of you. Thanks Marcus, really appreciate your time.

Marcus: Yep, talk Thursday.
