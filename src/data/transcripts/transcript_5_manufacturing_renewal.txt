SALES CALL TRANSCRIPT 5
========================
Industry: B2B Manufacturing / Industrial Equipment
Call Type: Renewal + Upsell Attempt
Outcome: Renewal secured, upsell deferred
Duration: 28 minutes
Rep: Claire Dupont
Prospect: Steve Kowalczyk, Plant Manager, Kowalczyk Metal Fabrication (family-owned, 90 employees)
========================

Claire: Steve, how are things on your end? Last time we spoke you were in the middle of that floor expansion.

Steve: Yeah, that's finally done. Took two months longer than planned, cost us more than we wanted, but it's done. We've got the new press line running.

Claire: That's great to hear. Is it performing the way you hoped?

Steve: It's good. We'll see once we're at full capacity. We've been ramping up gradually.

Claire: Makes sense. So the reason I wanted to get on today — your maintenance contract renews in six weeks. I wanted to go through it with you before it auto-renews, make sure it still fits where you are now.

Steve: Right. What's the pricing looking like for this year?

Claire: So your current contract is $28,400 a year. There's a 3.5% cost increase this year across all contracts, which takes it to $29,394. I know nobody likes to hear that, but I'll be straight with you — it's driven by parts costs on our end, and we haven't changed the service frequency or response time guarantees.

Steve: I figured there'd be something. Last year it went up 2%.

Claire: Yeah, it's been creeping. I won't pretend otherwise. What I can tell you is that your response time is still 4 hours guaranteed, and you've got unlimited callouts within the contract. Given you've had — let me check — four callouts this year, that's actually good value relative to what individual callouts would cost you.

Steve: Fair. The response time has been solid, I'll give you that. The tech you sent out in March — I think his name was Paulo — he was excellent. Knew his stuff.

Claire: I'll pass that on, he'll appreciate it. Okay, so the renewal itself I think makes sense for you. But while I have you — with the new press line running, have you thought at all about extending the contract coverage to include it?

Steve: I figured you'd bring that up.

Claire: [laughs] Guilty. It's new equipment, so the manufacturer's warranty covers you for the first 12 months on parts, but labour and diagnostics aren't included after the first 90 days. If something goes down on that line, you're looking at potentially significant downtime.

Steve: I know. But we've also just spent a lot of money on the expansion. Cashflow isn't exactly fat right now.

Claire: That's completely fair. What would help me understand is — if the new line went down for, say, 48 hours, what does that cost you in lost production?

Steve: [pause] Probably $30,000, maybe more. That line's going to be running three shifts once we're fully ramped.

Claire: So the coverage for the new equipment would be an additional $8,200 a year. That's protection against a $30,000 downtime event.

Steve: I get the logic, Claire. I do. But I need to get through Q3 before I take on more spend. Can we revisit it at the end of the year?

Claire: Absolutely. I'll put a note in my calendar for November and we can have a proper conversation about it then. By that point you'll also have a better sense of how the new line is performing.

Steve: That works. So for now, just the renewal at the new rate?

Claire: Exactly. I'll send you the updated agreement today for your signature. Should only take a few minutes to review — it's the same terms, just the adjusted price.

Steve: Fine. Send it over. I'll get it back to you this week.

Claire: Perfect. Thanks Steve. And tell your team good work on the expansion — that's no small thing.

Steve: Will do. Talk soon.
